# Evidence-Led Copywriting

Move the intended reader from a defined starting state to a useful destination without distorting the evidence or replacing the owner's point of view.

Read [the information-transfer model](../../references/information-transfer-model.md). Require a confirmed story contract; if it is missing, route back to `content-story-interview` rather than inventing a narrative.

## Brief check

Before prose, restate the declared content job, reader's prior knowledge, intended reader change, desired action, governing idea, proof burden, and format constraints. Resolve only material gaps; do not restart a confirmed interview.

## Draft

- Preserve the governing idea, owned tension, intended reader change, voice evidence, proof, and claim limits.
- Choose an order that serves this reader and content job. What it is, why it matters, why to believe it, how it works, and what to do are coverage questions, not a mandatory sequence. A procedure may lead with the action; a sourced personal story may follow the actual decision.
- Give each section one main argumentative job. A sentence may support more than one function, but it should not make the reader decode multiple unrelated claims.
- Check what relationship carries the reader between sections. Reorder or make a supported connection explicit; ask for missing owner reasoning rather than supplying a generic moral. Do not invent scenes for instructional copy.
- Put concrete evidence, scenes, mechanisms, and examples near the claims they support.
- Prefer the owner's recorded language when it is clear and appropriate; edit for comprehension without manufacturing mannerisms.
- Use necessary complexity. Remove friction, not nuance.
- Make hooks repayable by the body. Use urgency or scarcity only when confirmed and relevant.
- Separate platform adaptation from story invention. A shorter format may compress the contract but must not change it.

## Self-check

Run the `Huh?`, `Point at it`, `So what?`, `$10`, and destination tests from the information-transfer reference. Label all unresolved claims and editorial choices.

Return one primary draft, optional requested adaptations, a claim disposition, deviations from the story contract, and the next copy-approval gate. Never invent first-person experience, customer proof, results, numbers, specificity, or quotations.
